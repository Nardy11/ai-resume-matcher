Paste a CV and a job description. The same pipeline as the Python service runs in your browser: SBERT semantic similarity, TF-IDF keyword overlap, skill gaps and gendered-word flags. Your text is never uploaded.

How it works and what it can't do
  • Both texts are lower-cased and stripped of punctuation, then embedded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (first 256 tokens, mean pooled). Semantic similarity is the cosine of the two embeddings.
  • Keyword overlap is a TF-IDF cosine fitted on just these two texts, computed the way scikit-learn's TfidfVectorizer does by default.
  • Skills come from a fixed list of 16 (Python, Docker, SQL, PyTorch, ...). A skill counts as present when the text names it, or when one of its lines or sentences is within cosine 0.4 of the skill's embedding (so "trained CNNs in PyTorch" can count as deep learning). Skills outside the list are not seen at all.
  • The gendered-word check only looks for six pronouns and nouns. It does not detect coded language ("rockstar", "aggressive") and is a prompt for a human to look, not a verdict.
  • The scores rank how close two texts are. They say nothing about whether a person can do the job; do not use them to reject candidates automatically.
